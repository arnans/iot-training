# IoT Systems in Smart Farming — Session Overview

A summary of the three hands-on sessions (150 min each), broken down by activity, and how each activity serves the six learning goals. The full plans, timings and rationale are in [COURSE-DESIGN.md](COURSE-DESIGN.md); the participant worksheets are in [docs/](docs/).

## The six learning goals

| # | Learning goal | Core in | Applied / introduced in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explain the Input → Logic → Output model of autonomous systems | Session 1 | Every activity; recap in Session 3 |
| 2 | Choose sensors and actuators from specs, form factor and price | Session 1 | Session 3 design challenge |
| 3 | Create and implement control logic for smart-farm tasks | Session 2 | First automation at the end of Session 1 |
| 4 | Choose IoT infrastructure, communication and protocols | Session 3 | Seeded by the Session 2 failure lab |
| 5 | Apply redundancy, fail-safe, safety and maintenance principles | Session 2 | Session 1 control-box seed; Session 3 canvas rubric |
| 6 | Create dashboards to visualize inputs and control outputs | Session 3 | Session 1 history graph; Session 2 device pages only |

Each post-test targets the two goals that are core in its session.

## Session 1 — ระบบสมองกลฝังตัวในฟาร์ม: รับรู้ ตัดสินใจ สั่งการ

**Goals 1, 2 core · 3, 5, 6 introduced.** By the end of the session every team reads all sensors, commands the pump from laptop and phone, and has one automation that stops the pump when the tank runs dry.

| Activity | What the students do | Objective | Learning-outcome alignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before start** (setup) | Check the team's zone colour, join the zone's laptop WiFi, start the Home Assistant VM and log in, agree who does what, and write one sentence on the record sheet describing a farm problem they want to solve. | Get every laptop and kit ready without blocking the first activity, and plant the seed that Session 3's design challenge grows from. | No named roles: teams decide who does what and swap at every activity. The farm-problem sentence feeds Goal 2/4/5 in the Session 3 canvas. |
| **Live opener** (20 min) | Follow the Input → Logic → Output model through a fridge, a street light and a float-switch water tank, then see the same three boxes mapped onto the kit (sensor board = input, relay board = output, Home Assistant = logic + dashboard + logging) and a two-minute demo of the finished system. | Fix the three-box model and the vocabulary (Input / Logic / Output) that every later activity reuses verbatim. | **Goal 1** core: the water tank shows a system that already has all three parts, with mechanical logic. Goal 6 previewed in the demo. |
| **Activity 1 — Sense** (35 min) | Read the GoGo-IoT board's name and IP over USB at web.esphome.io, open the board's own web page, then find which entity moves when they breathe on the board, cup a hand over it, or lift the float switch out of the water (ON = Wet, OFF = Dry on the board's page). Count the seconds from covering to the light value changing. Sort the sensors into analog and digital. | Meet the input node directly, with no Home Assistant in the way, and learn what each sensor measures and which one the team will decide with. | **Goal 1** (the board is the Input). **Goal 2** through hands-on sensor behaviour and the analog vs digital distinction. The light-response delay feeds Session 2's chatter discussion. |
| **Activity 2 — Act** (40 min) | Part A: with nothing attached, toggle the relay from its web page and hear it click, see the LED, press the onboard button. Part B: wire the 12 V load circuit (charge controller → terminal block → relay → pump), power the relay board from the controller's USB port, run the pump from cup A to B once, then recirculate. Hear the instructor describe a real pump control box with an Auto / Off / Manual selector; note the 12 V vs 220 V safety rule. | See that a relay is just a switch, that a load needs its own supply, and wire a working output stage once by hand. | **Goal 1** (relay board is the Output). **Goal 2** through the load-to-relay-to-supply pairing. **Goal 5 introduced:** manual override as a standard, 12 V as the classroom voltage. |
| **Activity 3 — Logic** (40 min) | Adopt both boards into the team's Home Assistant by their six-character suffix. Click an entity to see its history graph, toggle the pump from laptop and phone. Be the Logic for five minutes: watch humidity, water by hand, lift the float switch out of the water mid-run so it reads Dry. Write two ถ้า…ให้… rules on the record sheet, then build the first automation: float reads Dry → relay off, and pass it 3 of 3. Then try the reverse order: hold the float switch out of the water first (already Dry), then start the pump. Nothing stops it, because the state never changed after the rule was armed. | Close the loop through software for the first time, and experience the difference between a human decision-maker and an automation before naming it. | **Goal 1** (Home Assistant is the Logic; "the board only knows now, HA remembers"). **Goal 3 introduced** with the first automation. **Goal 5** (float guard is a fail-safe). **Goal 6 introduced** with the history graph and phone control. The already-Dry puzzle is left open on purpose; it returns as sabotage card 4 in Session 2 and is fixed in Fail-safe 1. |
| **Activity 4 — Spec** (15 min, paper) | Draw a scenario card with a job stated in numbers, read the kit's specs (humidity and temperature accuracy, float-switch rating, relay DC rating, pump current), rule the kit fit or unfit, and name the one part to change. Hear the five things to read on every spec sheet. | Treat a spec sheet as a contract: a job beyond the spec cannot be done, however good the Logic. | **Goal 2** core: selecting by spec, form factor and rating. Feeds the priced canvas in Session 3. Note: this is a core outcome but currently the session's timing buffer. |
| **Post-test 1** (15 min, organizers) | Label Input / Logic / Output in three scenarios, pick a sensor from a spec, match loads to relays and supplies, spot the unsafe pairing, choose the Auto / Off / Manual position for a broken-sensor or leak scenario. | Individual evidence of Goals 1 and 2. | **Goals 1, 2** assessed; Goal 5 item included. |

## Session 2 — ตรรกะควบคุมอัตโนมัติและการออกแบบ Fail-safe สำหรับฟาร์ม

**Goals 3, 5 core · 6 extended · 4 seeded.** By the end of the session the light comes on by itself when dark without flickering, watering runs when the air is dry, and the pump stops itself when the tank is empty, a sensor disappears, it runs too long, or it was commanded on but is not actually running.

| Activity | What the students do | Objective | Learning-outcome alignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before start** (setup) | Re-join the zone WiFi, start the VM, read today's IP off the screen, confirm both boards' entities are back and the relay board's LED is solid. Refill cup A, put the pump and float back, rotate roles. Note: from now until lunch Home Assistant records everything, so the VM stays on. | Restore yesterday's system and start the half-day recording that Session 3 reads. | Sets up the Goal 6 evidence (own history) used in Session 3. |
| **Recap — automation has three parts** (15 min) | Open yesterday's float automation in the editor and name trigger / condition / action: a trigger answers "when did it change", a condition answers "what else is true right now". The team notes the three parts of the first rule. The Session 1 puzzle is not raised here; it returns as a sabotage card. | Give the team the three-part vocabulary they will use for every rule today, and confirm the system is back and recording. | **Goal 3** core: the anatomy of an automation, seen on a rule they already built. |
| **Activity 5 — Think** (75 min, including two load exchanges) | Disable the float rule, set the relay Off, and exchange the pump's two leads for the kit's 12 V LED module as the garden lamp (red lead to NO, black to the negative row). (a) Build a night-light automation on a single light threshold, following screenshot chips, and test it by crossing the threshold. Watch the lamp flicker as the value hovers; count the switching in one minute. (b) Add hysteresis: a second rule with a different off threshold, and count again. (c) Add a time-window condition, read a blocked run on the Traces page, then see that an already-dark sensor does not turn the lamp on when the window opens; the time-trigger fix and (d) a notification when time allows. Switch back to watering: disable the lighting rules, exchange the leads back, re-enable the float rule once the float reads Wet, then humidity threshold with hysteresis. Disable and re-enable a rule from the Automations page while commanding the relay from its device page. | Write rules that behave in the real world: threshold, deadband, condition, notification; know which rules are live at every step; and give the user a way to switch a rule off. | **Goal 3** core: (a), (b) and (c) tests required, the (c) fix and (d) stretch. **Goal 6** stays on device pages; the dashboard build is Session 3. |
| **Activity 6 — Failure lab** (45 min) | Four sabotage rounds from sealed cards, run by a representative of the paired team while the watering automation is live: sensor board switched off (entities go unavailable), laptop WiFi off (HA loses both boards), pump lead unscrewed from the relay's NO terminal (relay clicks, dashboard says on, no water moves), and the float switch lifted out of the water followed by a press of the relay board's onboard button (yesterday's rule stops the pump, then it runs again with the float still Dry: the Session 1 puzzle, staged). Observe, diagnose, reveal the cards. Then implement fail-safes: **1** upgrade the float rule in place with a notification, then close card 4 in two steps: add a float-Wet condition to the watering rule and test it with the switch held out of the water (pump must not start), then add a second trigger to the fail-safe rule (pump switch → On, condition float Dry) so a manual start from the dashboard or onboard button with no water is switched off within a second; **2** maximum pump run-time cutoff (30 s); **3** sensor unavailable → safe state; **4** (stretch) pump commanded on for 15 s with no vibration → off, using the sensor board's accelerometer. Name what is still unprotected: anything when HA itself is down. | Experience how an automation fails at each point in the chain (sensor, network, actuator), and make each fail-safe answer a failure the team watched. | **Goal 5** core: fail-safes 1 and 2 required, 3 and 4 when time allows; command ≠ result is the failure a dashboard cannot show. **Goal 3** reinforced (condition, second trigger, "for" duration). **Goal 4 seeded:** "the link died, the entities went unavailable, the pump was on its own"; the why waits for Session 3. |
| **Debrief and control-box demo** (15 min) | Discuss redundancy, watchdogs and maintenance: who checks the system weekly, what corrodes first. Watch the instructor flip a real Auto / Off / Manual box while the automation runs, then toggle the relay from its onboard button with Home Assistant shut down. Before lunch: leave the VM running, switch the watering automation off, set the relay Off. | See an override layer that lives below the Logic, and make maintenance part of the design. | **Goal 5** core: fail-safe must reach a safe state, not just notify, and should have a layer independent of the main Logic. Links back to the Session 1 control-box seed. |
| **Post-test 2** (15 min, organizers) | Predict what an automation does, fix a chattering automation with hysteresis, choose the fail-safe for a scenario, list maintenance items. | Individual evidence of Goals 3 and 5. | **Goals 3, 5** assessed. |

## Session 3 — โครงสร้างระบบ IoT: การสื่อสาร คลาวด์ แดชบอร์ด และการออกแบบระบบ

**Goals 4, 6 core · 2, 5 applied · 1 recap.** By the end of the session every team has a dashboard someone else can use, has seen their data from outside the farm, knows where an off-grid node gets its power, and has one system design of their own.

| Activity | What the students do | Objective | Learning-outcome alignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before start** (setup) | Confirm both boards' entities, switch the watering automation back on so the Session 2 system is complete, rotate roles. | Have the full watering system live for the dashboard and history work. | — |
| **Open the box: network** (20 min, live) | Read the wifi and api blocks in the ESPHome YAML (no key, no IP in the file), find the device IP on the integration page and the live log, revisit the board's own web page: "your board is a web server on this network". Then the four decision axes (distance, power, data rate, cost); WiFi, ESP-NOW, LoRa/LoRaWAN, cellular/NB-IoT, wired RS-485; MQTT, HTTP, ESPHome native API; local-first vs cloud, tied back to yesterday's WiFi-off card. | Explain the network the team has been using for two sessions, now that there is something concrete to explain, and give them the axes for choosing differently. | **Goal 4** core: infrastructure and protocol choice by worked decision, not by catalogue. Local-first vs cloud answers the Session 2 seed. |
| **Open the box: power** (20 min) | Read the charge controller's three terminal pairs and LCD (12.0 V never shows "full"), view the battery-type menu and leave it on lead-acid, connect the 15 W panel and compare charge current indoors vs by a window. Watch the instructor's projected demo of pulling the adapter with the panel connected: the system dies, because a panel without a battery is a night with no storage. | Know where a node outside the farm gets its power and what the controller can and cannot see. | **Goal 4** core: power source as one of the four axes. **Goal 1** recap: the charge controller is itself Input → Logic → Output. |
| **Our own half-day** (10 min) | Open History over the morning and find four things the team caused: the light dip from a cupped hand, fast relay toggling before hysteresis, gaps from the sabotage rounds, the pump's runs. Then answer why there is no night on the graph: the recorder went home in a laptop bag. | Read their own data, and see why a farm needs a machine that never closes its lid. | **Goal 6** core: interpreting logged data. **Goal 4:** motivates logging and an always-on local host. |
| **Activity 7 — Dashboard** (45 min) | Design on paper for a named real user first, then build: gauges, history (now populated with the morning), controls, automation status, mobile view. Swap laptops with the neighbouring team for a peer test. Then push kit data to a cloud dashboard or broker for remote access, following the instructor on screen (tool and procedure to be finalised). | Build a dashboard a farmer can read without knowing the numbers, and see the same data from outside the workshop network. | **Goal 6** core: full farm dashboard with controls and automation status, peer-tested. **Goal 4:** cloud push and remote access; the remote outcome is optional until the tool is chosen. |
| **Activity 8 — Design challenge** (45 min) | Draw a scenario card (greenhouse / 10-rai orchard / hydroponics / remote plot with no mains) or use a member's own farm from the Session 1 sentence. Fill the printed canvas: sensors and actuators with real prices, comms and protocol choice with reasoning, at least one fail-safe, and a pilot plan (benefit, measure, first trial, owner, review date). 30 min design, 5 min paired pitch with two feedback questions, 10 min plenary with three or four teams nominated by their pair. | Turn the course into a costed, justified design for a real plot that the team takes home as the first draft of their own system. | **Goals 2, 4, 5 applied**, scored on the rubric: spec justified? price realistic? comms reasoning sound? fail-safe present? |
| **Wrap-up** (10 min) | Review the three boxes across the three sessions, take the where-to-go-next sheet with the take-home install guide, fill in the feedback form, return the kit in the stated disconnect order, keep the record sheet. | Close the story and send the team home with a working Home Assistant on their laptop. | **Goal 1** recap. |
| **Post-test 3** (15 min, organizers) | Choose comms for three farms (backyard greenhouse, 10-rai orchard, remote field with no mains), dashboard and local-vs-cloud items. The design canvas is scored separately. | Individual evidence of Goals 4 and 6. | **Goals 4, 6** assessed; canvas covers 2, 4, 5. |

## How the sessions connect

- **One kit, one story.** The same two boards, pump and charge controller run through all three sessions. Session 1 gets them working, Session 2 makes them decide and survive failure, Session 3 explains the parts that were hidden and asks the team to design their own.
- **Experience first, vocabulary second.** Teams run a networked system on Day 1 without configuring an address; the network and power boxes are opened only in Session 3, when there is something concrete to explain.
- **Hooks carried forward.** The already-Dry float puzzle (Session 1 → Session 2 sabotage card 4 → Fail-safe 1), the WiFi-off sabotage card (Session 2 → 3), the one-sentence farm problem on the record sheet (Session 1 → 3) and the missing night in the history graph (the laptop went home) each pay off in a later session.
- **Everyone swaps at every activity.** There are no named roles (decided 8 Sep 2026): each team decides who does what, swaps at each activity boundary so everyone touches both the laptop and the kit, and answers one written question per activity, so no member of a team of four is a spectator.

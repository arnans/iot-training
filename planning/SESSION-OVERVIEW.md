# IoT Systems in Smart Farming — Session Overview

A summary of the four hands-on sessions (150 min each; Session 4 added 20 Sep 2026), broken down by activity, and how each activity serves the six learning goals. The full plans, timings and rationale are in [COURSE-DESIGN.md](COURSE-DESIGN.md); the participant worksheets are in [docs/](../docs/).

## The six learning goals

| # | Learning goal | Core in | Applied / introduced in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explain the Input → Logic → Output model of autonomous systems | Session 1 | Every activity; recap in Session 3 |
| 2 | Choose sensors and actuators from specs, form factor and price | Session 1 | Session 3 design challenge |
| 3 | Create and implement control logic for smart-farm tasks | Session 2 | First automation at the end of Session 1 |
| 4 | Choose IoT infrastructure, communication and protocols | Session 3 | Seeded by the Session 2 failure lab |
| 5 | Apply redundancy, fail-safe, safety and maintenance principles | Session 2 | Session 1 control-box seed (the Session 3 canvas rubric was cut on 18 Sep 2026) |
| 6 | Create dashboards to visualize inputs and control outputs | Session 3 | Session 1 history graph; Session 2 device pages only |

Each post-test targets the two goals that are core in its session.

## Session 1 — ระบบสมองกลฝังตัวในฟาร์ม: รับรู้ ตัดสินใจ สั่งการ

**Goals 1, 2 core · 3, 5, 6 introduced.** By the end of the session every team reads all sensors, commands the garden lamp from laptop and phone, and has two automations that switch the lamp on when dark and off when bright. The pump waits for Session 2 (revised 14 Sep 2026).

| Activity | What the students do | Objective | Learning-outcome alignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before start** (setup) | Check the team's zone colour, join the zone's laptop WiFi, start the Home Assistant VM and log in, agree who does what, and write one sentence on the record sheet describing a farm problem they want to solve. | Get every laptop and kit ready without blocking the first activity, and plant the seed that Session 3's design challenge grows from. | No named roles: teams decide who does what and swap at every activity. The farm-problem sentence feeds Goal 2/4/5 in the Session 3 design step (8.1 and 8.6). |
| **Live opener** (20 min) | Follow the Input → Logic → Output model through a fridge, a street light and a float-switch water tank, then see the same three boxes mapped onto the kit (sensor board = input, relay board = output, Home Assistant = logic + dashboard + logging) and a two-minute demo of the finished system. | Fix the three-box model and the vocabulary (Input / Logic / Output) that every later activity reuses verbatim. | **Goal 1** core: the water tank shows a system that already has all three parts, with mechanical logic. Goal 6 previewed in the demo. |
| **Activity 1 — Sense** (35 min) | Read the GoGo-IoT board's name and IP over USB at web.esphome.io, open the board's own web page, then find which entity moves when they breathe on the board, cup a hand over it, or lift the float switch out of the water (ON = Wet, OFF = Dry on the board's page). Count the seconds from covering to the light value changing. Sort the sensors into analog and digital. | Meet the input node directly, with no Home Assistant in the way, and learn what each sensor measures and which one the team will decide with. | **Goal 1** (the board is the Input). **Goal 2** through hands-on sensor behaviour and the analog vs digital distinction. The light-response delay feeds Session 2's chatter discussion. |
| **Activity 2 — Act** (40 min) | Part A: with nothing attached, toggle the relay from its web page and hear it click, see the LED, press the onboard button. Part B: wire the 12 V load circuit (charge controller → terminal block → relay → the kit's 12 V LED module), power the relay board from the controller's USB port, light the lamp on command, aim its lenses away from the sensor board. Hear the instructor describe a real pump control box with an Auto / Off / Manual selector; note the 12 V vs 220 V safety rule. | See that a relay is just a switch, that a load needs its own supply, and wire a working output stage once by hand — on a load whose result is visible from across the table. | **Goal 1** (relay board is the Output). **Goal 2** through the load-to-relay-to-supply pairing. **Goal 5 introduced:** manual override as a standard, 12 V as the classroom voltage. |

Write two ถ้า…ให้… rules, then build them: `มืด-เปิดไฟ` (light crossed below X → relay on) and `สว่าง-ปิดไฟ` (above the same X → relay off), and pass 3 of 3 cover/uncover rounds. The threshold-hover question is left open on purpose; Session 2 opens by measuring the chatter and fixing it with hysteresis. The float is observed in Activity 1 only; its rule is built in the Session 2 failure lab as Fail-safe 1. |

## Session 2 — ตรรกะควบคุมอัตโนมัติและการออกแบบ Fail-safe สำหรับฟาร์ม

**Goals 3, 5 core · 6 extended · 4 seeded.** By the end of the session the light comes on by itself when dark without flickering, the pump is wired and watering runs when the air is dry, and the pump stops itself when the tank is empty, a sensor disappears, it runs too long, or it was commanded on but is not actually running.

| Activity | What the students do | Objective | Learning-outcome alignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before start** (setup) | Four checks, reported to the dashboard as one activity: Home Assistant is up, the sensor board shows values, the relay board switches the LED, and both boards and Home Assistant stay on for the half-day recording. The TA also confirms the two lighting rules are on and the optional timer is off, and that today's VM IP has been read off the screen. | Restore the Session 1 system and start the half-day recording that Session 3 reads. | Sets up the Goal 6 evidence (own history) used in Session 3. |
| **Activity 4 — Think** (70 min, including the one load exchange) | (a) The two Session 1 rules share one threshold: waver a hand at it and count the lamp's switching in one minute. (b) Add hysteresis: move the off rule's threshold and count again. (c) Add a time-window condition, read a blocked run on the Traces page, then see that an already-dark sensor does not turn the lamp on when the window opens, and add the time-trigger fix. (d) A persistent notification. Then wire the pump for the first time: disable the lighting rules and the timer, exchange the LED's leads for the pump's, put the float in its glass, run the pump dry for ten seconds, record the changeover time, then humidity threshold with hysteresis. Then a software Auto / Manual switch: a Toggle helper `Pump Auto Mode` on a first (auto-populated) dashboard, added as a condition to the pump-on rule, tested off and on with the blow-and-wait loop while the device page still runs the pump by hand. | Write rules that behave in the real world: threshold, deadband, condition, notification; know which rules are live at every step; and give the user a way to switch a rule off. | **Goal 3** core: (a)–(d) all required since 14 Sep 2026 (the lamp swap and the dark→on build moved to Session 1). **Goal 6** stays on device pages; the dashboard build is Session 3. |
| **Activity 5 — Failure lab** (55 min) | Four problems, each in three steps: **sabotage** (a member of the paired team performs it while our team watches only the Home Assistant screen), **fix**, **sabotage again** to confirm. Every problem states how to sabotage, what happens if the sabotage succeeds, and how to tell it is fixed. 1: float lifted, then the relay board's button pressed — the pump runs with the float Dry and nothing watches the float at all → Fail-safe 1 builds the float rule from scratch (trigger float→Dry, second trigger pump→On with condition float Dry, relay off + notification) and adds a float-Wet condition to the watering rule. 2: pump lead unscrewed from NO — relay clicks, screen says On, nothing moves → Fail-safe 4, commanded On for 15 s with no vibration → off (moved up from fourth on 16 Sep 2026: a lead the paired team can actually unscrew). 3: sensor board switched off — entities go unavailable, pump keeps running, no rule reacts (only Fail-safe 2 eventually, for the wrong reason) → Fail-safe 3, unavailable/unknown → pump off. 4: pump started by hand and left alone runs forever → Fail-safe 2, 30 s run-time cutoff, last because it has nothing to sabotage. The laptop-off-WiFi round was cut on 16 Sep 2026 (Roger); every fail-safe going silent when Home Assistant cannot reach the board is now read off the summary table and answered in the end-of-activity question and the control-box demo. Record per problem: sabotage worked? / what happened / fixed? / note. Ends with the maintenance table (who checks what, how often), moved in from the debrief on 16 Sep 2026. | Experience each failure in the chain (sensor, network, actuator, controller) before building the rule that answers it, and prove the fix by repeating the same sabotage. | **Goal 5** core: all four problems required since 16 Sep 2026; command ≠ result is the failure a dashboard cannot show. **Goal 3** reinforced (condition, second trigger, "for" duration). **Goal 4 seeded:** "the link died, the entities went unavailable, the pump was on its own"; the why waits for Session 3. |
| **Wrap-up** (10 min) | Read the five learning points. Before lunch: leave the VM running, switch the watering automation off, set the relay Off, download answers. The discussion bullets and the control-box demo left the worksheet on 16 Sep 2026; the demo is instructor-run from the run sheet when it fits. | Know that the fail-safes end with the controller, and leave the kit safe for lunch. | **Goal 5** core: fail-safe must reach a safe state, not just notify, and should have a layer independent of the main Logic. Links back to the Session 1 control-box seed. |
| **Post-test 2** (15 min, organizers) | Predict what an automation does, fix a chattering automation with hysteresis, choose the fail-safe for a scenario, list maintenance items. | Individual evidence of Goals 3 and 5. | **Goals 3, 5** assessed. |

## Session 3 — โครงสร้างระบบ IoT: การสื่อสาร คลาวด์ แดชบอร์ด และการออกแบบระบบ

**Goals 4, 6 core · 2, 5 applied · 1 recap.** By the end of the session every team can read its own recorded data and say what caused each feature, has a dashboard someone else can use, and has one system design of its own in which the network and the power source were chosen by elimination and by arithmetic, then improved by another team's questions. The afternoon opens with the two hands-on blocks; after the break the whole slot is the design challenge, with the network and power "boxes" opened as decision steps inside it (restructured 8–9 Sep 2026).

## Session 4 — ขยายระบบไปแปลงที่ WiFi ไม่ถึง: ESP-NOW และบอร์ดตัวรับ

**Goal 4 core · 5, 6 applied · 1 recap.** Added 20 Sep 2026. Session 3's network step chose a technology by elimination; this session builds one. By the end every team's GoGo board broadcasts its readings over ESP-NOW, a second ESP32-C3 in the "farmhouse" hears it and serves the values by IP, Home Assistant shows the far plot on a dashboard, and an automation says when the plot goes quiet. The pump is not used; every relay-driving rule stays off. In the room the GoGo board keeps its WiFi so the two paths can be compared and the board can be re-flashed; the worksheet says a real plot board would have none.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Before start** (setup) | Five checks at the top of Activity 9: kit powered and float in its glass, Home Assistant reachable, both boards online, all relay-driving rules and `Pump Auto Mode` off, receiver devkit received but not plugged in. | Restore the Session 3 system and take the pump out of play. | — |
| **Activity 9 — Send** (30 min) | Download the zone's `gogo-iot-<colour>.factory.bin` and install it on the GoGo board at web.esphome.io (**Install**, the same tool as Activity 1). ## How the sessions connect

- **One kit, one story.** The same two boards, pump and charge controller run through all four sessions. Session 1 gets them working, Session 2 makes them decide and survive failure, Session 3 explains the parts that were hidden and asks the team to design their own, and Session 4 adds one plain board and builds the network Session 3 only reasoned about.
- **Experience first, vocabulary second.** Teams run a networked system on Day 1 without configuring an address; the network and power boxes are opened only in Session 3, when there is something concrete to explain.
- **Hooks carried forward.** The threshold-hover question (Session 1 Activity 3 → Session 2 chatter count → hysteresis), the limits of Home-Assistant-side fail-safes (Session 2 summary table → 3), the one-sentence farm problem on the record sheet (Session 1 → 3) and the missing night in the history graph (the laptop went home) each pay off in a later session.
- **Everyone swaps at every activity, on the instructor's cue.** There are no named roles (decided 8 Sep 2026): each team decides who does what, the instructor announces a swap at each activity boundary so everyone touches both the laptop and the kit (the worksheet carries no swap callouts, 9 Sep 2026), and the team answers one written question per activity, so no member of a team of four is a spectator.

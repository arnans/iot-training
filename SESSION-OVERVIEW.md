# IoT Systems in Smart Farming — Session Overview

A summary of the three hands-on sessions (150 min each), broken down by activity, and how each activity serves the six learning goals. The full plans, timings and rationale are in [COURSE-DESIGN.md](COURSE-DESIGN.md); the participant worksheets are in [docs/](docs/).

## The six learning goals

| # | Learning goal | Core in | Applied / introduced in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explain the Input → Logic → Output model of autonomous systems | Session 1 | Every activity; recap in Session 3 |
| 2 | Choose sensors and actuators from specs, form factor and price | Session 1 | Session 3 design challenge |
| 3 | Create and implement control logic for smart-farm tasks | Session 2 | First automation at the end of Session 1 |
| 4 | Choose IoT infrastructure, communication and protocols | Session 3 | Seeded by the Session 2 failure lab |
| 5 | Apply redundancy, fail-safe, safety and maintenance principles | Session 2 | Session 1 control-box seed; Session 3 canvas rubric |
| 6 | Create dashboards to visualize inputs and control outputs | Session 3 | Session 1 history graph; Session 2 device pages only |

Each post-test targets the two goals that are core in its session.

## Session 1 — ระบบสมองกลฝังตัวในฟาร์ม: รับรู้ ตัดสินใจ สั่งการ

**Goals 1, 2 core · 3, 5, 6 introduced.** By the end of the session every team reads all sensors, commands the garden lamp from laptop and phone, and has two automations that switch the lamp on when dark and off when bright. The pump waits for Session 2 (revised 14 Sep 2026).

| Activity | What the students do | Objective | Learning-outcome alignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before start** (setup) | Check the team's zone colour, join the zone's laptop WiFi, start the Home Assistant VM and log in, agree who does what, and write one sentence on the record sheet describing a farm problem they want to solve. | Get every laptop and kit ready without blocking the first activity, and plant the seed that Session 3's design challenge grows from. | No named roles: teams decide who does what and swap at every activity. The farm-problem sentence feeds Goal 2/4/5 in the Session 3 canvas. |
| **Live opener** (20 min) | Follow the Input → Logic → Output model through a fridge, a street light and a float-switch water tank, then see the same three boxes mapped onto the kit (sensor board = input, relay board = output, Home Assistant = logic + dashboard + logging) and a two-minute demo of the finished system. | Fix the three-box model and the vocabulary (Input / Logic / Output) that every later activity reuses verbatim. | **Goal 1** core: the water tank shows a system that already has all three parts, with mechanical logic. Goal 6 previewed in the demo. |
| **Activity 1 — Sense** (35 min) | Read the GoGo-IoT board's name and IP over USB at web.esphome.io, open the board's own web page, then find which entity moves when they breathe on the board, cup a hand over it, or lift the float switch out of the water (ON = Wet, OFF = Dry on the board's page). Count the seconds from covering to the light value changing. Sort the sensors into analog and digital. | Meet the input node directly, with no Home Assistant in the way, and learn what each sensor measures and which one the team will decide with. | **Goal 1** (the board is the Input). **Goal 2** through hands-on sensor behaviour and the analog vs digital distinction. The light-response delay feeds Session 2's chatter discussion. |
| **Activity 2 — Act** (40 min) | Part A: with nothing attached, toggle the relay from its web page and hear it click, see the LED, press the onboard button. Part B: wire the 12 V load circuit (charge controller → terminal block → relay → the kit's 12 V LED module), power the relay board from the controller's USB port, light the lamp on command, aim its lenses away from the sensor board. Hear the instructor describe a real pump control box with an Auto / Off / Manual selector; note the 12 V vs 220 V safety rule. | See that a relay is just a switch, that a load needs its own supply, and wire a working output stage once by hand — on a load whose result is visible from across the table. | **Goal 1** (relay board is the Output). **Goal 2** through the load-to-relay-to-supply pairing. **Goal 5 introduced:** manual override as a standard, 12 V as the classroom voltage. |
| **Activity 3 — Logic** (40 min) | Adopt both boards into the team's Home Assistant by their six-character suffix. Check the sensors on the device page and toggle the lamp from laptop and phone. On the flower-farm night-light scenario, pick a light threshold halfway between the open and covered readings. Write two ถ้า…ให้… rules, then build them: `มืด-เปิดไฟ` (light crossed below X → relay on) and `สว่าง-ปิดไฟ` (above the same X → relay off), and pass 3 of 3 cover/uncover rounds. Fast teams add `ปิดไฟตามเวลา`, a time trigger at a minute of their choosing, test it and switch it off. Written question: what do the two rules do when the reading hovers at the threshold? | Close the loop through software for the first time. | **Goal 1** (Home Assistant is the Logic). **Goal 3 introduced** with the first two automations and, optionally, a time trigger. **Goal 6 introduced** with phone control (the history graph and the "be the Logic" round were cut 15 Sep 2026; Session 3 opens on History). The threshold-hover question is left open on purpose; Session 2 opens by measuring the chatter and fixing it with hysteresis. The float is observed in Activity 1 only; its rule is built in the Session 2 failure lab as Fail-safe 1. |
| **Spec game** (removed 15 Sep 2026) | The scenario-card spec game that closed Session 1 as Activity 4 is out of the worksheets (no time); it is archived in `archive/session-1-activity-4-spec.md` with restore steps. The Session 2 recap now carries the number 4, so the worksheets run Activities 1–8 without a gap. | — | **Goal 2**'s selecting-by-spec is now assessed in post-test 1 and applied in the Session 3 canvas only. |
| **Post-test 1** (15 min, organizers) | Label Input / Logic / Output in three scenarios, pick a sensor from a spec, match loads to relays and supplies, spot the unsafe pairing, choose the Auto / Off / Manual position for a broken-sensor or leak scenario. | Individual evidence of Goals 1 and 2. | **Goals 1, 2** assessed; Goal 5 item included. |

## Session 2 — ตรรกะควบคุมอัตโนมัติและการออกแบบ Fail-safe สำหรับฟาร์ม

**Goals 3, 5 core · 6 extended · 4 seeded.** By the end of the session the light comes on by itself when dark without flickering, the pump is wired and watering runs when the air is dry, and the pump stops itself when the tank is empty, a sensor disappears, it runs too long, or it was commanded on but is not actually running.

| Activity | What the students do | Objective | Learning-outcome alignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before start** (setup) | Re-join the zone WiFi, start the VM, read today's IP off the screen, confirm both boards' entities are back and the relay board's LED is solid, the LED module is still on the relay, the two lighting rules are on and the optional timer is off. Note: from now until lunch Home Assistant records everything, so the VM stays on. | Restore the Session 1 system and start the half-day recording that Session 3 reads. | Sets up the Goal 6 evidence (own history) used in Session 3. |
| **Activity 4 — Recap: automation has three parts** (15 min) | Open the Session 1 lamp automation `มืด-เปิดไฟ` in the editor and name trigger / condition / action: a trigger answers "when did it change", a condition answers "what else is true right now". The team notes the three parts of the first rule. | Give the team the three-part vocabulary they will use for every rule today, and confirm the system is back and recording. | **Goal 3** core: the anatomy of an automation, seen on a rule they already built. |
| **Activity 5 — Think** (70 min, including the one load exchange) | (a) The two Session 1 rules share one threshold: waver a hand at it and count the lamp's switching in one minute. (b) Add hysteresis: move the off rule's threshold and count again. (c) Add a time-window condition, read a blocked run on the Traces page, then see that an already-dark sensor does not turn the lamp on when the window opens, and add the time-trigger fix. (d) A persistent notification. Then wire the pump for the first time: disable the lighting rules and the timer, exchange the LED's leads for the pump's, set up the water and the float in container A, run A → B once and recirculate, record the changeover time, then humidity threshold with hysteresis. Disable and re-enable a rule from the Automations page while commanding the relay from its device page. | Write rules that behave in the real world: threshold, deadband, condition, notification; know which rules are live at every step; and give the user a way to switch a rule off. | **Goal 3** core: (a)–(d) all required since 14 Sep 2026 (the lamp swap and the dark→on build moved to Session 1). **Goal 6** stays on device pages; the dashboard build is Session 3. |
| **Activity 6 — Failure lab** (50 min) | Five problems, each in three steps: **sabotage** (a member of the paired team performs it while our team watches only the Home Assistant screen), **fix**, **sabotage again** to confirm. Every problem states how to sabotage, what happens if the sabotage succeeds, and how to tell it is fixed. 1: float lifted, then the relay board's button pressed — the pump runs with the float Dry and nothing watches the float at all → Fail-safe 1 builds the float rule from scratch (trigger float→Dry, second trigger pump→On with condition float Dry, relay off + notification) and adds a float-Wet condition to the watering rule. 2: pump started by hand and left alone runs forever → Fail-safe 2, 30 s run-time cutoff. 3: sensor board switched off — entities go unavailable, pump keeps running, no rule reacts (only Fail-safe 2 eventually, for the wrong reason) → Fail-safe 3, unavailable/unknown → pump off. 4: pump lead unscrewed from NO — relay clicks, screen says On, nothing moves → Fail-safe 4, commanded On for 15 s with no vibration → off. 5: laptop WiFi off — every fail-safe goes silent, pump runs on; no fix exists inside Home Assistant, which is the end-of-activity question and the control-box demo. Record per problem: sabotage worked? / what happened / fixed? / note. | Experience each failure in the chain (sensor, network, actuator, controller) before building the rule that answers it, and prove the fix by repeating the same sabotage. | **Goal 5** core: problems 1, 2 and 5 required, 3 and 4 when time allows; command ≠ result is the failure a dashboard cannot show. **Goal 3** reinforced (condition, second trigger, "for" duration). **Goal 4 seeded:** "the link died, the entities went unavailable, the pump was on its own"; the why waits for Session 3. |
| **Debrief and control-box demo** (15 min) | Discuss redundancy, watchdogs and maintenance: who checks the system weekly, what corrodes first. Watch the instructor flip a real Auto / Off / Manual box while the automation runs, then toggle the relay from its onboard button with Home Assistant shut down. Before lunch: leave the VM running, switch the watering automation off, set the relay Off. | See an override layer that lives below the Logic, and make maintenance part of the design. | **Goal 5** core: fail-safe must reach a safe state, not just notify, and should have a layer independent of the main Logic. Links back to the Session 1 control-box seed. |
| **Post-test 2** (15 min, organizers) | Predict what an automation does, fix a chattering automation with hysteresis, choose the fail-safe for a scenario, list maintenance items. | Individual evidence of Goals 3 and 5. | **Goals 3, 5** assessed. |

## Session 3 — โครงสร้างระบบ IoT: การสื่อสาร คลาวด์ แดชบอร์ด และการออกแบบระบบ

**Goals 4, 6 core · 2, 5 applied · 1 recap.** By the end of the session every team can read its own recorded data and say what caused each feature, has a dashboard someone else can use, and has one system design of its own in which the network and the power source were chosen by elimination and by arithmetic, then improved by another team's questions. The afternoon opens with the two hands-on blocks; after the break the whole slot is the design challenge, with the network and power "boxes" opened as decision steps inside it (restructured 8–9 Sep 2026).

| Activity | What the students do | Objective | Learning-outcome alignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before start** (setup) | Confirm both boards' entities, switch the watering automation back on so the Session 2 system is complete. | Have the full watering system live for the history and dashboard work. | — |
| **Our own half-day** (20 min) | Open History since the start of Session 2 and find three points of the team's own choosing that it can link to something it did in Session 1 or 2 (the scripted events are examples, not required finds, since not every graph will hold them). For each: what, when, what we were doing, and what a farm would check or change on seeing it unattended. Relay On is a command count, never a pump-run count. Then answer why there is no lunch break on the graph: the recorder went home in a laptop bag. | Read their own data, draw a farm decision from it, and see why a farm needs a machine that never closes its lid. | **Goal 6** core: interpreting logged data. **Goal 4:** motivates logging and an always-on local host. |
| **Activity 7 — Dashboard** (35 min) | Plan on paper for a named real user (5), build the minimum — gauge, float tile, pump tile, automations list — then graph, colours and icons if time allows (25), and hand the phone to someone from the paired team for a three-question usability test (5). | Build a dashboard a farmer can read without knowing the numbers, and prove it on a stranger. | **Goal 6** core: farm dashboard with controls and automation status, peer-tested. |
| **Activity 8 — Design challenge** (85 min) | Pick a scenario card or a member's own farm (5). **Network step (20, live):** see the wifi/api blocks and the board's own web page, then choose the farm's local network by elimination — distance first, then power at the far end — for the two links in the team's own scenario, against a short reference table of WiFi, ESP-NOW, Zigbee, LoRa, cellular and RS-485 (range figures given live since 15 Sep 2026); then decide whether the owner needs access from outside at all, choosing from a menu of four ways (notifications only, Home Assistant Cloud, VPN, pushing data to an IoT cloud; "opening a door yourself" was cut from the worksheet 15 Sep 2026), with the instructor demonstrating Home Assistant Cloud as one of them. **Power step (15):** read the charge controller at the team's own box, then size panels and batteries for four loads by two simple rules and discover that a farm pump belongs on direct daylight solar with a water tank, while sensors get a small panel and a small battery. **Fill the canvas (30)** as a first pilot with the dated price sheet; **paired exchange (10)** on two questions; **revise one thing (5)**. No plenary. | Turn the course into a costed, justified first trial for a real plot in which the network and power choices are reasoned, not copied from the classroom. | **Goals 2, 4, 5 applied**, scored on the rubric: spec justified? price realistic? comms chosen by elimination? power per point sensible? fail-safe present? gaps named? **Goal 4** core is delivered here. |
| **Wrap-up** (10 min) | Review the three boxes across the three sessions, take the where-to-go-next sheet with the take-home install guide and fill in the feedback form (both handed out or projected; the worksheet's shopping table and feedback QR were cut 15 Sep 2026), return the kit in the safe order (automations off, relay Off, power isolated, then drain), keep the record sheet. | Close the story and send the team home with a working Home Assistant on their laptop. | **Goal 1** recap. |
| **Post-test 3** (15 min, organizers) | Choose comms for three farms (backyard greenhouse, 10-rai orchard, remote field with no mains), dashboard, local-vs-cloud and explain-remote-access items. The design canvas is scored separately. | Individual evidence of Goals 4 and 6. | **Goals 4, 6** assessed; canvas covers 2, 4, 5. |

## How the sessions connect

- **One kit, one story.** The same two boards, pump and charge controller run through all three sessions. Session 1 gets them working, Session 2 makes them decide and survive failure, Session 3 explains the parts that were hidden and asks the team to design their own.
- **Experience first, vocabulary second.** Teams run a networked system on Day 1 without configuring an address; the network and power boxes are opened only in Session 3, when there is something concrete to explain.
- **Hooks carried forward.** The threshold-hover question (Session 1 Activity 3 → Session 2 chatter count → hysteresis), the WiFi-off problem (Session 2 → 3), the one-sentence farm problem on the record sheet (Session 1 → 3) and the missing night in the history graph (the laptop went home) each pay off in a later session.
- **Everyone swaps at every activity, on the instructor's cue.** There are no named roles (decided 8 Sep 2026): each team decides who does what, the instructor announces a swap at each activity boundary so everyone touches both the laptop and the kit (the worksheet carries no swap callouts, 9 Sep 2026), and the team answers one written question per activity, so no member of a team of four is a spectator.
